# Yardr Launch Strategy

## Phase 1: Pre-Launch (Weeks 1-4)

### App Store Readiness
- **Polish remaining features** — finalize video AI integration, Stories feature, and search/filtering
- **App Store assets** — screenshots, preview video, app description, keywords (target: "garage sale finder", "yard sale near me", "estate sale app")
- **TestFlight / Google Play Internal Testing** — get 20-30 beta testers from your target area (Saskatoon initially)
- **Fix bugs from beta feedback** before public launch

### Build a Waitlist & Social Presence
- **Landing page** (yardr.app or similar) with email signup + App Store "Notify Me"
- **Instagram & TikTok accounts** — post short-form content:
  - "Best garage sale finds" compilations
  - Behind-the-scenes of the AI video feature ("point your camera, get a listing in seconds")
  - Tips for hosting a great garage sale
- **Facebook Groups** — join local buy/sell/trade and garage sale groups in Saskatoon; don't spam, but engage and tease the app

### Local Partnerships
- Reach out to **community centers, churches, and neighborhood associations** that host multi-family sales
- Partner with **local resellers/thrift influencers** for early access

---

## Phase 2: Soft Launch (Weeks 5-8) — Saskatoon Focus

### Launch in One City First
- **Geo-focused launch in Saskatoon** — easier to build critical mass in one area
- Seed the app with **10-20 real garage sale listings** so the map doesn't feel empty on day one
- Target **garage sale season** (spring/summer) — timing is everything for this app

### Growth Tactics
- **"List Your Sale Free" campaign** — make it dead simple for sellers to post; the AI video feature is your differentiator
- **Yard signs + flyers** — physical marketing at actual garage sales: "Find more sales like this on Yardr" with QR code
- **Referral program** — "Share with a friend, both get featured listing placement"
- **Local media** — pitch to Saskatoon local news/blogs: "Local app uses AI to make garage sales easier"

### Metrics to Track

| Metric | Week 5 Target | Week 8 Target |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| Downloads | 200 | 1,000 |
| Active listings | 20 | 100 |
| Weekly active users | 50 | 300 |
| Retention (D7) | 25% | 30% |

---

## Phase 3: Growth & Expansion (Months 3-6)

### Expand to More Cities
- **Prioritize cities with active garage sale culture** — Regina, Calgary, Edmonton, Winnipeg
- Use the same playbook: seed listings, local partnerships, targeted social ads

### Feature Additions for Retention
- **Search & advanced filters** (price range, categories, distance)
- **Seller ratings & reviews** — build trust
- **"Garage Sale Route Planner"** — optimize a driving route hitting multiple sales
- **Social sharing** — let buyers share their best finds

### Monetization (Start Testing)
- **Featured Listings** — $2-5 to pin your sale at the top of Discover
- **Promoted Sales** — push notifications to nearby users for a fee
- **"Pro Seller" subscription** — unlimited listings, analytics, multi-day sales support
- Start with small tests; don't paywall core features early

---

## Phase 4: Scale (Months 6-12)

### Go National (Canada → US)
- US garage sale market is massive — target suburbs in midwest/south first
- **ASO (App Store Optimization)** — optimize for "yard sale" (US term) vs "garage sale"

### Community & Virality
- **"Garage Sale Weekend" events** — coordinate community-wide sales through the app
- **User-generated content** — let buyers post haul photos/videos
- **Seasonal campaigns** — spring cleaning = listing season, back-to-school = buying season

### Fundraising (if needed)
- By this point you should have enough data (DAU, retention, revenue) to pitch investors
- Comparable exits: Nextdoor, OfferUp, Mercari all started hyper-local

---

## Key Differentiators to Emphasize in All Marketing

1. **AI-powered listing in seconds** — point your camera, get a complete listing (no other garage sale app does this)
2. **Map-first discovery** — see what's near you instantly
3. **Push notification reminders** — never miss a sale
4. **Free for sellers** — zero friction to list

---

## Key Risk: Chicken-and-Egg Problem

The biggest risk for a marketplace app is the chicken-and-egg problem (no buyers without listings, no listings without buyers). That's why launching hyper-local in Saskatoon and manually seeding listings is critical. Once you prove the model in one city, expansion becomes a repeatable playbook.
